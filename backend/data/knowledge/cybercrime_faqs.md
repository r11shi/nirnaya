# Cybercrime FAQs and Common Scam Patterns

## 1. What is the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal?
The National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (cybercrime.gov.in) is an initiative of the Government of India to facilitate victims/complainants to report cybercrime complaints online. You can also call the national helpline number **1930** to report financial fraud and stop the flow of funds.

## 2. Remote Access App Scam
Fraudsters often pose as bank officials or technical support and ask you to download apps like AnyDesk, TeamViewer QuickSupport, or Screenshare. Once installed, these apps give the fraudster complete access to your phone, allowing them to read OTPs and make unauthorized transactions. **Never install a screen-sharing app at the request of a stranger.**

## 3. The "Accidental Money Transfer" Scam
A fraudster sends you a fake SMS showing money credited to your account (e.g., ₹5,000). They then call you, claiming they sent it by mistake, and ask you to return the money via a UPI ID. If you check your actual bank statement, you will see no such credit was made. The fake SMS is just a trick to get you to send your own money.

## 4. Fake Customer Care Numbers
Fraudsters optimize fake numbers on Google Search for banks, airlines, or e-commerce companies. When a customer calls these numbers for help, the fraudster answers and asks the customer to install a screen-sharing app, share card details, or pay a small "processing fee" via a link, which leads to identity theft or financial loss. Always use the official website or app to find contact details.

## 5. Parcel/Customs Scam (FedEx/DHL)
Victims receive a call claiming a parcel sent in their name contains illegal goods (e.g., passports, drugs) and is held by customs or police. The caller impersonates a customs officer or CBI official and demands a "penalty" or "clearance fee" to avoid arrest. Genuine courier companies and law enforcement agencies do not demand money over phone calls to settle cases.

## 6. Fake Loan App Scam
Victims download unverified loan apps offering instant loans without credit checks. These apps ask for extensive permissions (contacts, gallery). The app may disburse a small amount but charge exorbitant interest. If the victim fails to pay, the fraudsters use the victim's contacts and photos to harass and blackmail them.

## 7. Immediate Actions if Defrauded
1. **Call 1930 immediately.** The sooner you call, the higher the chance of freezing the fraudulent transaction.
2. Register a complaint on cybercrime.gov.in.
3. Call your bank's customer care to block your account, debit card, and UPI.
4. Do not delete any evidence (SMS, WhatsApp chats, call logs, transaction IDs).
